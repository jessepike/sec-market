## Market question

> Are agents becoming a consequential cybersecurity action surface quickly and credibly enough that organizations with material exposure should move from **investigate** to **prepare now**?

This is a market-timing and applicability question. It tests whether runtime and agentic controls are becoming necessary **alongside** data discovery and posture, not whether posture is being replaced. It is not a vendor-selection exercise, an AI-security ontology project, or a request to choose a universal platform.

## Decision supported

Determine the warranted posture for security leadership across the present-to-24-month horizon:

- **Prioritize:** allocate material resources or initiate an active program now.
- **Prepare:** establish ownership, requirements, architecture readiness, and bounded validation now.
- **Investigate:** monitor and conduct limited inquiry without readiness investment.
- **Defer:** take no near-term action beyond ordinary monitoring.
- **No-call:** evidence cannot support a directional posture.

The posture may differ by exposure. A universal call is not required.

## Working hypothesis

**Hypothesizing:** Agents with tool, write, or sensitive-data access are creating an additional runtime control surface. Organizations with current or planned material exposure may need bounded preparation now, while organizations without that exposure may remain in investigate. Product maturity and customer adoption may not justify broad platform standardization or material procurement.

## Signal families

1. **Enterprise demand and adoption**
   - Reported deployment of AI agents and enterprise AI applications
   - Security-leadership priorities, budgets, projects, and operating ownership
   - Public customer deployments and operational outcomes

2. **Product and control maturity**
   - Generally available versus preview or announced controls
   - Inventory, posture, runtime enforcement, tool-action governance, monitoring, response, and evidence
   - Cross-provider and cross-environment coverage

3. **Capital allocation and consolidation**
   - Acquisitions, funding, and platform integration
   - Whether established security platforms are absorbing specialist capabilities

4. **Standards and regulatory pressure**
   - Requirements affecting AI inventory, data use, accountability, monitoring, human oversight, and evidence
   - Technical standards or shared control patterns for agents and runtime behavior

5. **Ecosystem and infrastructure controls**
   - Model-provider, cloud, browser, endpoint, gateway, identity, and tool-protocol enforcement points
   - Whether runtime control is becoming native infrastructure rather than only a specialist product

6. **Independent operating evidence**
   - Reconstructable production outcomes, limitations, false-block rates, latency, bypass resistance, and failure behavior
   - Independent evidence that distinguishes adoption from product announcements

## Evidence rules

- Preserve original source origin when reporting is repeated.
- Separate product availability, vendor claims, customer testimony, independent reporting, and independent operating evidence.
- Treat funding and acquisition activity as investment signals, not proof of demand or control efficacy.
- Treat preview, beta, early-access, and roadmap features as directional rather than operational.
- Do not convert an absence of public evidence into evidence of absence.
- Map technical findings to CISO Advisory Core IDs where useful, but do not reproduce or modify its ontology or catalog.

## Falsifiers

The working hypothesis weakens or fails if:

- runtime and agentic controls remain predominantly preview, beta, or acquisition roadmaps;
- independent customer evidence remains sparse while announcements accelerate;
- enterprise use remains concentrated in copilots without material autonomous tool or data access;
- exposed organizations can meet identity, authorization, evidence, interruption, and recovery requirements through ordinary use-case review without any distinct readiness work;
- native model and cloud controls make a separate cross-platform readiness motion unnecessary;
- security leaders continue to solve the material risk through established data, identity, application, and cloud controls without a distinct agentic layer;
- product overlap, integration immaturity, or false-block risk makes near-term preparation more costly than ordinary monitoring; or
- demand-side evidence does not move with supply-side investment.

## Anti-proxy controls

- Announcement volume is not market adoption.
- Funding and valuation are not customer demand.
- A common “agentic” label is not equivalent control coverage.
- A vendor feature matrix is not a timing judgment.
- Regulatory mappings are not operational compliance.
- A polished report is not success unless it changes or confirms a leadership posture.
