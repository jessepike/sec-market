How this is built

How this is built

The market-by-domain tracker publishes facts only: no stances, rankings, forecasts, or sentences interpreting what a fact means. “Revenue growth” is the year-over-year change in a company’s own reported GAAP revenue for the quarter named, taken from its SEC filings or its own press release; where a company reports no comparable revenue figure, the table says so rather than estimating one. Facts are collected, checked against the sourcing ladder below, and published automatically — no human approval step, and no machine judgment. This edition is the Episode 002 packet (SEC-MARKET-SIG-002 v0.2) plus a facts-only pass, updated September 26, 2026. Every number links to a source excerpt and its limits.

The sourcing ladder

Every distress or consolidation fact on the tracker is checked against this ladder before it can publish. A fact resting only on the weakest rung, a single trade-press report with no primary document behind it, does not publish on this site — it stays a lead until a stronger source confirms it.

The five-rung sourcing ladder, strongest first
RungWhat it isPublishes alone?
1. Company's own SEC filingAn 8-K, 10-Q or 10-K filed with the SEC and readable on EDGAR.Yes
2. Rating agency action, republishedA Moody's, S&P or Fitch rating action, most reliably read on the rated company's own newsroom.Yes
3. State WARN Act noticeA mass-layoff or closure notice filed with a state workforce agency.Yes
4. Confirmed primary corporate actionAn acquirer's own press release, or a company or parent's own wind-down statement.Yes
5. Trade press reportA news report, including one citing unnamed sources, with no primary document behind it.No

How the tracker ages

Current prints stay on the scorecard. Older still-true context collapses to Background. Superseded prints leave the card and stay in evidence. Acquired vendors keep their last independent print — we do not rewrite them into the buyer. Tracked figures are updated as companies report; the run status near the top of the page says whether the latest run was complete.

How that archived call was reasoned (thesis only)

  1. 1

    Separate the fact from the story

    Keep the excerpt, date, period, and metric definition. Interpretation sits next to it, not on top of it.

  2. 2

    Do not collapse signal types

    Realized revenue, organic growth, guidance, intent, funding, and acquisitions stay in different buckets.

  3. 3

    Need breadth before a domain call

    One strong print is a card. A domain posture needs more than one company and a reason the motion is not just share shift or M&A.

  4. 4

    Refuse the lag if the window cannot support it

    A stable infra-to-security lag needs two mature buildout impulses and a comparable security response. This window has one mature impulse. That is a no-call, not a guessed number of quarters.

  5. 5

    Action follows exposure, not the composite

    PREPARE only where the account has a matching live motion. A split market is not a license for a broad campaign, and this episode does not authorize RUN.

  6. 6

    Age the edition; do not pretend it is live

    Keep the latest comparable print on the card. Demote still-true context. Leave superseded prints in evidence. Freeze acquired entities. Refresh on earnings, material capital events, and falsifiers — not on a news clock.

  7. 7

    Date the posture; show the delta

    Every domain posture carries the date it was set, the horizon it governs, and the evidence that produced it. Each edition states what held or moved versus the prior edition. Do not overwrite the last state.
