## Market question

> Is the current infrastructure and AI-capex cycle producing a measurable leading signal for increased cybersecurity demand, over what horizon, and which security domains are likely to capture it first?

## The call

> **The infrastructure cycle is still accelerating. A stable infrastructure-to-security lag is not measurable from the current public window. Do not run a broad cybersecurity-boom motion. Use infrastructure activation as a qualification trigger and prepare only bounded, domain-specific readiness where a client or account has relevant exposure.**

The responsible posture is:

- **PREPARE** account-level readiness only where cloud/AI capacity, sensitive data, machine identity, network refresh, or SASE displacement is becoming operational;
- **INVESTIGATE** cloud/CNAPP and identity/NHI as market calls because comparable public financial coverage is insufficient;
- **PREPARE** selectively in data/AI security and network/SASE only when account conditions match the evidence;
- **INVESTIGATE** SecOps platform shifts and AI-runtime security until organic, independently supported demand becomes clearer; and
- **DO NOT claim** that broad cybersecurity spending is already following infrastructure on a stable timetable.

## What the sequence shows

### 1. Compute moved first

NVIDIA’s Data Center revenue moved from $4.28 billion in the quarter ended 2023-04-30 to $75.25 billion in the quarter ended 2026-04-26. That is the clearest opening signal, but one supplier is not a broad infrastructure cycle.

### 2. The cycle broadened in 2024

The common impulse became visible when:

- hyperscaler capex accelerated across Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, and Oracle;
- Dell began reporting a scaled AI-server backlog;
- Cisco declared customer destocking largely behind it and began reporting AI-infrastructure orders; and
- Arista and later Vertiv showed networking, power, and thermal demand joining the build.

This broad 2024 impulse is the appropriate starting point for a lag test.

### 3. Total cybersecurity budgets did not follow immediately

The strongest buyer-side series shows:

- 6 percent average security-budget growth in 2023;
- 8 percent in 2024;
- 4 percent in 2025; and
- security’s share of IT falling from 11.9 percent in 2024 to 10.9 percent in 2025 as AI and cloud IT spending rebounded.

That weakens any claim of a zero-to-four-quarter broad spending response.

### 4. Selected security signals strengthened later, for different reasons

By 2025–2026:

- **Realized network/SASE momentum:** Fortinet accelerated; Zscaler maintained low-to-mid-twenties organic growth; Cisco firewall orders improved.
- **Selected data-security momentum:** Rubrik and Varonis accelerated on their own defined metrics.
- **Vendor-specific recovery:** CrowdStrike re-accelerated after a disruption and commitment-package period.
- **Non-pure platform corroboration:** Cloudflare accelerated across security, connectivity, developer, and compute activity.
- **Intent and strategic capital:** buyer intent shifted toward GenAI protection and identity, while Wiz and CyberArk attracted major platform capital.

These observations do not share one proven cause. Check Point’s weak growth, Cisco’s 2 percent full-year security growth, and Okta’s deceleration prevent a broad category call.

### 5. The second infrastructure impulse remains unobserved downstream

The late-2025 and 2026 step-up in Vertiv orders, Dell backlog, Cisco AI orders, and hyperscaler capex is still converting into capacity. Public statements describe multi-year data-center lead times and continuing capacity constraints. A downstream security response to that impulse is right-censored at the research freeze.

## Why the lag remains a no-call

A stable lag requires at least two mature infrastructure episodes followed through a comparable security response. The current window has:

- one broad mature impulse beginning in 2024;
- one later impulse that has not matured;
- only fourteen aligned quarters at most;
- six or fewer raw pairs at an eight-quarter lag;
- acquisitions and business-model transitions across several security vendors;
- incompatible capex, ARR, RPO, billings, and order definitions; and
- independent budget data that is annual rather than quarterly.

Selecting the largest correlation from this window would manufacture precision. The evidence supports a sequence, not a constant.

## Domain timing

### Cloud security and CNAPP — INSUFFICIENT COVERAGE

Why:

- cloud and AI-capacity investment is broad and continuing;
- IANS identifies cloud security among the first incremental budget priorities;
- cloud and data incidents expose real control gaps;
- the CNAPP category formed early and moved into consolidation; and
- Alphabet allocated $32 billion to Wiz.

The market call remains **INVESTIGATE** because there is no clean three-company domain-specific financial series. For accounts activating cloud or AI capacity, bounded readiness is still appropriate:

- maintain a current cloud-security and CNAPP point of view;
- tie conversations to workload migration, AI-capacity activation, multicloud, and deployment milestones;
- distinguish native cloud controls from residual specialist gaps; and
- avoid treating the Wiz transaction as proof of customer spend by itself.

### Identity and non-human identity — INSUFFICIENT COVERAGE

Why:

- identity is a recurring requirement across cloud, AI, regulatory, and insurance evidence;
- non-human identity ranks near the top of buyer requirements for agentic workloads;
- the CyberArk transaction represents major platform capital allocation; and
- incidents continue to expose credential, MFA, and delegated-access weaknesses.

Counterweight:

- Okta’s cRPO and revenue growth remain positive but decelerated;
- many baseline identity controls are being bundled or required as hygiene; and
- agent-identity product revenue remains early.

The market call remains **INVESTIGATE**. For accounts introducing consequential machine identities or delegated access, use identity as a qualification and architecture-readiness topic, not a claim that spending has broadly inflected.

### Data and AI security — PREPARE FOR EXPOSED ACCOUNTS

Why:

- Rubrik and Varonis show selected organic acceleration;
- cloud and AI incidents increase data-governance pressure;
- AI-security spending intent increased materially; and
- funding and product formation are strong.

Counterweight:

- public histories are short or transition-distorted;
- DSPM may be absorbed into CNAPP, privacy, or platform controls; and
- planned AI-protection spend has not yet become a broad realized series.

### Network, firewall, and SASE — PREPARE WHERE CURRENT CHANGE EXISTS

Why:

- Fortinet product revenue and billings accelerated;
- Cisco reported strong firewall and core-security orders;
- Zscaler maintained solid organic growth; and
- Cloudflare accelerated.

Counterweight:

- firewall refresh timing, pricing, platformization, and vendor share shifts explain part of the movement;
- Check Point remained weak, while Cisco improved to 14 percent Q4 growth but only 2 percent for FY2026 and still embeds Splunk; and
- networking demand can be infrastructure spending rather than incremental cybersecurity demand.

Treat this as a current demand and refresh motion. Do not relabel it as proof that AI capex caused a broad security wave.

### SecOps and analytics — INVESTIGATE

Splunk, Red Canary, Chronosphere, and other acquisitions create strategic activity but break financial comparability. Hiring remains constrained, and buyer evidence favors automation rather than headcount. The evidence supports platformization and share movement more clearly than a new broad SecOps demand cycle.

### AI runtime security — INVESTIGATE

Product formation and buyer intent are strong, but independent customer outcomes and realized specialist spend remain limited. Episode 001 remains useful thematic evidence, not proof of a market-wide financial inflection.

## Strongest alternative

> Infrastructure and selected security signals may be moving because of common or independent drivers—not because infrastructure spending leads cybersecurity spending.

That can occur if:

- cloud migration, regulation, incidents, refresh cycles, pricing, acquisitions, and vendor share rotation can independently strengthen security categories;
- hyperscalers and infrastructure platforms can absorb security into native offerings;
- enterprise AI remains in pilots rather than scaled production;
- cloud and security budgets are constrained by the cost of infrastructure itself;
- platform consolidation redistributes existing security spend;
- the largest AI contracts remain concentrated among frontier labs and hyperscalers; or
- security demand appears as services, governance, or operational change rather than new software categories.

The alternative remains plausible. It would be strengthened if security domains move with regulation, incidents, refresh dates, or acquisitions rather than with a future registered infrastructure impulse. It would weaken only when the registered impulse and response conditions pass while those independent drivers do not change.

### Next 2–4 quarters

Look for:

- realized security-budget growth and a rebound in security share of IT;
- organic acceleration across a majority of comparable security vendors;
- expansion from AI pilots into consequential production workloads;
- evidence that native controls leave measurable residual gaps; and
- distributor or buyer evidence that converts category intent into bookings.

### Next 5–8 quarters

The late-2025 and 2026 infrastructure impulse may become mature enough to test, but no lag band is pre-judged. If scaled workloads increase while security budgets and organic vendor breadth remain flat, the lag thesis weakens toward native absorption, independent drivers, or no downstream response.

## Falsifiers and posture changes

### Strengthen the lag thesis

- a future registered infrastructure impulse is followed by a registered security response under the fixed cohorts, metrics, formulas, quorums, and definition-change rules; and
- buyer corroboration passes either the IANS rule (both budget growth and security share of IT rise) or the two-source rule (two method-consistent realized-spend sources each improve by at least 2 percentage points).

One pair supports one-cycle sequencing only. Stable-lag validation requires a second prospective pair in the same broad band.

### Weaken the lag thesis

- AI and cloud deployment scale while security budgets remain flat or lose further share;
- native platform security captures the requirement without specialist expansion;
- selected security acceleration disappears after adjusting for acquisitions and refresh cycles;
- infrastructure demand rolls over before workloads deploy; or
- a future registered infrastructure impulse receives no registered security response inside eight quarters.

## New evidence version triggers

Open a new frozen evidence version when:

- all core 2026-Q2 security companies have reported;
- the next IANS security-budget benchmark is published;
- two complete quarters satisfy the pre-registered supplier and hyperscaler breadth condition; or
- a material guidance cut, cancellation, buyer-budget shock, or regulatory event challenges the cycle.

Procurement, standardization, or a practice-scale GTM commitment requires a separate exposure-specific decision episode with realized buyer need and measured residual gaps.

## No-call areas

The frozen evidence does not support:

- an exact infrastructure-to-security lag;
- a causal coefficient or forecast derived from cross-correlation;
- one security domain capturing all downstream spend;
- one vendor winning a domain;
- a broad security-budget acceleration as of the freeze;
- which security domain will capture downstream demand first;
- causal attribution of any domain’s growth to infrastructure capex;
- treating acquisitions as organic category growth;
- treating capex as deployed capacity; or
- treating this analysis as investment advice.

## Collection mechanics worth preserving

The manual episode identified stable mechanics that could later enter human-verified shadow mode after independent review:

- period-end normalization across fiscal calendars;
- separate publication date and economic period;
- canonical capture of revenue, capex, guidance, orders, backlog, ARR, RPO, and billings definitions;
- explicit acquisition and metric-definition adjustments;
- layer separation between suppliers, hyperscalers, security vendors, and buyers;
- category-shift versus share-shift checks; and
- visible `INSUFFICIENT COVERAGE` and `NO-CALL` states.

No autonomous posture judgment or production pipeline is authorized.

## Decision

**Use infrastructure activation as a qualification trigger; do not claim a measured market-wide lag or broad cybersecurity boom.**

The infrastructure build is real and still accelerating. Security evidence is mixed across refresh, recovery, platformization, intent, and selected organic growth. Use the cycle to ask better account questions and prepare reversible domain views where exposure exists. Demand realized buyer spend and broad organic vendor evidence before scaling a market-wide GTM motion.
