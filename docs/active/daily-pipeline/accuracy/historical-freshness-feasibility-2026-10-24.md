The current strict rule requires fresh transport for all 9,914 retained source/URL obligations before all 17 sources can pass. It does not require a new model review of every unchanged page. The existing regular job cannot sustain those checks within its allowance, even with ideal sharing. Continue the authorized repairs and honest degraded operation; an owner decision is needed only to change what whole-source success establishes.

## Hard limit and operational limit

The pinned policy has a 24-hour interval, 30-hour source/receipt age, 5,500 fetch reservations per UTC day, 40 new document reservations and 80 model calls. Selection processes at most two URLs per source invocation, including at most one old/undated audit URL. That is a per-invocation bound, not a persistent two-per-day source ledger. Multiple forced invocations are technically possible; they do not solve the sustained fetch-rate limit.

There are 9,276 unique article URLs. Exactly 638 Thoma Bravo URLs are shared by Exabeam and Sophos. Current ordinary transport remains supplier-isolated, requiring 9,914 article requests per rolling 30 hours, or at least 7,931.2/day on average. Even ideal safe full-200 sharing needs 7,420.8/day, above 5,500 before any index or other task. The actual index inventory adds 98 source contexts / 97 unique URLs: 10,012 current transport keys or 9,373 ideal shared keys. The latter requires 7,498.4/day. A full allowance supports at most 6,875 distinct contexts per rolling 30h on a sustained average basis.

Two UTC-day allowances can theoretically place up to 11,000 reserved requests inside one 30-hour interval. That prevents claiming a universal one-off impossibility from fetch arithmetic alone. It does not sustain the average rate, prove that processing fits, or justify repeated forced runs as the daily solution.

## What implementation can save

The archive contains 4,543 latest successful raw source/URL checks for this inventory; only 43 were within 30 hours at the pinned coverage time. Of those raw checks, 3,530 carry an ETag or Last-Modified hint. Conditional GET can save transfer bytes, and exact unchanged input/body/policy context can reuse semantic proof. It still requires a charged, real successful request. No cache stamp or index visit renews an old fetchedAt.

There are exactly 24 current-policy document-validation files for these suppliers, matching the separately pinned rebinding cohort: eight reviewed-empty, six complete candidate-disposition and ten deterministic entity-exclusion records. This audit checked their observation/body references and dates; it did not repeat the companion audit’s full semantic replay. Rebinding them saves models and preserves history, but completes none of these source tasks.

Do not equate 9,890 unresolved URLs with 9,890 necessary new model calls. Cached identical inputs and valid no-entity screening can require zero new model/document reservations. The actual new semantic cost cannot be known without proper body/context checks. The 40-document limit applies to new extraction reservations, while the two-selected bound also restricts cache/exclusion/freshness work in the current path.

Safe entity screening already applies to shared investor/acquirer or distributor pages using full readable text and the exact target entity. It cannot exclude by URL/title absence. The malformed Sophos alias needs proper repair before broad exclusions can be trusted. Even if all 4,532 shared-publisher contexts cost no models, fresh transport remains required. Strict ordinary 200 sharing could save duplicated raw requests with separate per-target provenance and review; its optimistic saving is already granted in the bound above.

A proved first-party current/archive boundary can improve source qualification and future intake. It cannot erase known history, turn sitemap modification into publication, or create an ordinary-web immutable-history exception. No inspected proof permits that shortcut under today’s full-history 30-hour rule. No cap increase or denominator reduction is recommended.

## Decision surface critique

The proposed history-and-daily-coverage-decision-2026-10-24.md correctly keeps old dates and separates intake, current processing and historical review. Add the sustained-fetch bound above; the per-invocation selection limit alone is not the hard impossibility argument. Replace its unconditional promise that separate daily monitoring can work within the allowance with a requirement to measure actual new/changed/undated intake, full pagination, semantic cost and current-event recall. Those rates are not proved here.

A fresh complete index may establish an intake result. It cannot establish complete current-event processing while relevant new/changed/undated documents remain unreviewed, mark them quiet, or refresh old facts/certification. A changed definition needs an explicit policy version and separate measured baseline; the old 244/557 records retain their original meaning. Ordinary cache, conditional-fetch, rebinding and qualified-source repairs remain authorized without a new owner decision.

The strongest countercase is that selection is per invocation and much work can become zero-model cache/exclusion replay. That can accelerate an initial audit or allow a one-off fresh window. It cannot defeat the sustained lower bound, which already assumes zero new model work and ideal transport sharing. Actual primary proof reducing required fresh checks without losing obligations would change the conclusion; it is absent here.
