## Fit-for-purpose blockers

### Blocker 1 — Factual meaning: FAIL

Checked every lane headline and every scorecard figure label on `/`, `/issues/2026-w36/`, and
the Platform Pressure table against the evidence/source text shown beside it, not a sample.

**Finding A — company mislabelled, three times.** The "Platform pressure by vendor position"
table's "Buying in rather than building" row repeats the judged-read sentence "Cloud security
absorbed into a hyperscaler. Watch what it does to the specialist gap." for four acquisitions.
It is correct once (Wiz, acquired by Google, a real hyperscaler) and wrong three times:

- "Protect AI ... Palo Alto · $634.5M · closed Jul 2025 ... Cloud security absorbed into a
  hyperscaler." — Palo Alto Networks is a security vendor, not a hyperscaler.
- "CalypsoAI ... F5 · $145.2M · closed Sep 2025 ... Cloud security absorbed into a
  hyperscaler." — F5 is not a hyperscaler.
- "SurePath AI ... F5 · $50.1M · closed Jun 2026 ... Cloud security absorbed into a
  hyperscaler." — same error.

This is a copy-pasted boilerplate line applied to acquirers it does not describe. Elsewhere on
the same page the same three deals are correctly attributed to Palo Alto Networks and F5 by
name (e.g. the "M&A" edition-delta entry, the Activity board's "Background — last disclosed
raises"), so the site knows the correct acquirer; the Platform Pressure table's interpretive
line does not reflect it.

**Finding B — two figures remain mislabelled by period, with a correction beside them, not
in them.** The corrections record (`/corrections/`, `corrections.json`) states plainly: "two
headlines gave a quarterly revenue figure as a full-year figure. Zscaler's revenue of $898.2
million and Palo Alto Networks' revenue of $3.41 billion are each for the fiscal fourth
quarter ended 31 July 2026, not for fiscal year 2026." On `/issues/2026-w36/` and on `/`, the
original headlines are still shown exactly as published — "Zscaler FY2026 revenue grows to
$898.2M..." and "Palo Alto FY2026 revenue grows 34% to $3.41B..." — each followed by a
"CORRECTED 20 SEP 2026" block with the accurate wording and a "See correction" link, per the
site's stated policy that a published issue is never edited, only annotated.

**Reading applied.** The rubric's own instruction anticipates exactly this shape of case: "a
wrong headline with a dated correction beside it should be judged as the rubric's wording
dictates." Blocker 1's wording is "No figure on the page is wrong or mislabelled... period,"
with no carve-out for an annotated correction. Read literally, the two "FY2026" headlines are,
as displayed, wrong on the period axis, and the correction sitting beside them does not change
what is printed in the headline itself. Applying that literal reading, and combined with
Finding A (which no correction addresses at all), **blocker 1 fails.** The correction record
itself is exemplary practice and is credited in D2, D5 and D12; it does not cure the figures
still on the page.

### Blocker 2 — Comparison validity: FAIL

Every sentence on `/` and `/issues/2026-w36/` that ranks, orders, or describes suppliers as
leading, accelerating, or slowing:

- "Net new ARR accelerated to 51% year-over-year, the strongest reacceleration among this
  week's prints." (CrowdStrike) — compares CrowdStrike's own metric's rate of change against
  other companies' revenue/ARR rates of change. The underlying bases differ (net-new ARR
  dollars vs. revenue and ARR growth percentages) but the comparison is of growth-rate
  magnitude generally, and is not flagged as a problem by the site itself. Judged as within
  normal editorial range; not counted as a failure on its own.
- "Next-Gen Security ARR growth of 63% is the fastest among this week's public-issuer
  prints." (Palo Alto, in the original, uncorrected headline block) — this is the exact
  comparison the site's own correction withdraws: "Next-Gen Security ARR is a company-defined
  segment and is not comparable with other companies' total revenue growth, so the comparison
  is withdrawn." The withdrawal is real and clearly written. But the sentence being withdrawn
  is still printed on the page, immediately above the withdrawal, exactly as blocker 2
  describes: a ranking made "on the strength of figures that are not like for like."
- "Data Center revenue growth accelerated to 117% year-over-year, the strongest print in this
  week's set." (NVIDIA) — same pattern as the CrowdStrike line above; not counted as a failure
  on its own for the same reason.
- "Cato Networks at +43% and Netskope at +31% are printing faster in the same category" than
  Zscaler organic ARR at +21% (appears in the domain snapshot, the Landscape section, and the
  Platform Pressure table). The three figures are, respectively, "2025 ARR" (full calendar
  year 2025), "ARR (annual), FY26" and "organic ARR, APR '26" — three different vintages and
  one explicitly ex-acquisition ("organic") basis. The site labels each figure's metric and
  period individually next to the number, and flags the older two as "older figure," which is
  the disclosure practice the rubric rewards elsewhere. Judged as adequately caveated in place;
  not counted as a failure.

**Reading applied.** As with blocker 1, the literal wording controls: the withdrawn "fastest
among this week's public-issuer prints" sentence is still present on the page. **Blocker 2
fails** on that sentence alone. The Cato/Netskope-vs-Zscaler comparison, despite spanning three
different reporting vintages, is judged to pass because each figure is individually labelled
with its own metric, period, and vintage flag beside it — the disclosure the rubric asks for
elsewhere in D5 and D1.
