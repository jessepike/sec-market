# Blind review — Security Market Pulse front-page redesign

You are one of four independent reviewers. Four designers each built a redesign of the Security Market
Pulse front page from the same brief. They are labelled A, B, C and D. You do not know who made which,
and you must not try to find out.

**Read only:**
- the brief: `/Users/jessepike/code/projects/sec-market/docs/active/design-bakeoff/brief.md`
- the purpose statement: `/Users/jessepike/code/projects/sec-market/docs/active/product-core.md`
- the four designs: `/Users/jessepike/code/projects/sec-market/docs/active/design-bakeoff/review/designs/{A,B,C,D}/`. Each has
  `index.html` (the working prototype), `shot-fold-1440.png` and `shot-fold-390.png` (the first screen
  at desktop and phone width), and `shot-1440.png` and `shot-390.png` (the full page).

Do not open any other file or folder under `docs/archive/2026-10-refresh-gate/design-bakeoff/`, and do not read the other
reviewers' files. Do not modify anything except your own review file.

**Review each design** as a demanding product designer would, from the point of view of the reader
the brief names: a chief information security officer opening the page for the first time. Look at the
screenshots and read the prototype's HTML to check the data, the interactions and the rules. Look
specifically for breaches of the hard constraints in brief section 4. The most important is any
machine-made judgment: a ranking, a stance, a good-or-bad colour, a default order that implies a
ranking, or interpretive wording. Also look for figures shown without a source or period, invented or
altered figures, a dark theme, horizontal scroll at 390, and the owner's name in the hero.

**Score each design from 1 to 10** on these five criteria, which are the brief's section 7 in order of
weight:
1. First screen: does a security leader get the picture of the market in the first screen?
2. Look: is it a pleasure to look at (calm, modern, easy on the eyes)?
3. Density: is it dense with real information, through structure rather than cramming?
4. Constraints: does it keep every hard constraint? A clear breach caps this score at 4.
5. Phone: does it work well at 390 pixels?

**Write your review** to the file path you were given. Use this structure, in plain sentences:

- A table with one row per design (A to D), the five scores, and a weighted total, computed as
  first screen × 3 + look × 3 + density × 2 + constraints × 2 + phone × 1, for a maximum of 110.
- For each design: two to four sentences on its strongest quality, two to four on its biggest
  weakness, and any constraint breaches, each with the exact place it appears.
- Your winner and runner-up, with a short reason for each.
- What to borrow: the one or two specific things from each of the other designs that would improve
  your winner.
- The five most important fixes your winner needs before it ships.

Be specific and concrete. Name sections, elements and wording. Do not praise in general terms.
