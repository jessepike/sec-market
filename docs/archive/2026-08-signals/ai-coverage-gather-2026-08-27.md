# AI security coverage — gather record

**ID:** `SEC-MARKET-AI-001` Part 2 · **Ran:** 2026-08-25 / 2026-08-27 · **Status:** complete, lead-verified

Findings only. Nothing here has been published, and no posture, market call, or confidence value
has been changed on the strength of it. Presentation is a separate decision the spec holds open
until coverage is known. Coverage is now known.

## What ran

Four bounded lanes across twelve public companies, plus a verification lane against a list of ten
rumoured AI security acquisitions. A first attempt was abandoned when the agents fanned out into
sub-agents and exhausted the session budget; the re-run banned sub-agent spawning, replaced open
sweeps with fixed four-company target lists, and required each lane to record "none disclosed" and
move on rather than keep hunting. Every figure below that carries a filing reference was then
re-extracted from the filing by the lead, not accepted on a lane's report.

## Finding 1 — no company discloses buyer-side AI security revenue. Twelve for twelve.

Microsoft, Zscaler, SentinelOne, Varonis, Palo Alto Networks, CrowdStrike, Cisco, Check Point,
Okta, SailPoint, CyberArk, and F5 were each checked across investor-relations pages, newsrooms,
and their two most recent quarterly filings. Not one discloses revenue, ARR, customer count, seat
count, or attach rate for an AI security product.

What exists instead is qualitative. Varonis cites "customer interest in securing AI." SailPoint
says it has "begun to experience accelerating demand across our AI and machine identity portfolio."
CyberArk's last earnings release attributes record net-new ARR of $99M partly to "human, machine,
and agentic AI identities," but that $99M is total-company net-new ARR with no AI carve-out. Where
these products appear in a filing at all, they appear as product description folded into an
aggregate line — Palo Alto's Next-Generation Security ARR of $8.1B, for instance, which mixes
Prisma AIRS with everything else the company sells.

Two scoping notes that stop this being over-read. The sweep covered releases, filings, and
newsrooms; it did not cover earnings-call transcripts, which is where the site's existing Prisma
AIRS figure of 300-plus customers comes from. That figure is not contradicted — it is simply not
repeated in writing. And Okta's "more than 20,000 customers" is its overall single-sign-on base,
not an AI-agent figure: Agent SSO ships free inside existing plans and is not counted separately.

## Finding 2 — the filings do carry hard evidence, in the acquisition accounting

This is the part worth publishing. Two acquirers state in audited filings both what they paid for
an AI security company and that the acquired business's results were immaterial, and both allocate
almost the whole price to goodwill with nothing assigned to customer relationships.

**F5 / CalypsoAI.** Closed 2025-09-26 for $145.2M in cash. The purchase allocation is other net
tangible assets $14.2M, developed technology $16.9M, goodwill $114.2M. The filing states: "The pro
forma financial information, as well as the revenue and earnings generated by CalypsoAI, were not
material to the Company's operations for the periods presented." There is no customer-relationships
intangible. F5's 8-K at announcement said $180M; the 10-Q at closing says $145.2M cash. Both are
SEC-filed. Cite the closing figure and name the discrepancy rather than picking silently.

**F5 / SurePath AI.** Closed 2026-06-15 for $50.1M, "a provider of network-based AI discovery,
intent classification, and shadow AI detection." Goodwill $39.1M of the $50.1M. "The acquisition
did not have a material impact to the Company's operating results." This deal was on no public
list checked in this gather and surfaced only from reading the filing.

**Palo Alto Networks / Protect AI.** Closed 2025-07-22 for $634.5M ($607.4M cash plus $27.1M in
replacement awards). Allocation: goodwill $515.8M, identified intangible assets $70.0M — all of it
developed technology, over five years — cash acquired $50.5M, net liabilities assumed $(1.8)M. The
filing states: "Pro forma results of operations have not been presented because the effects of the
acquisitions were not material to our consolidated statements of operations." That sentence covers
the fiscal year's acquisitions collectively rather than naming Protect AI alone, which is weaker
than F5's wording and should be quoted as such.

**The contrast that makes it legible.** The same Palo Alto filing reports the IBM QRadar
transaction, where identified intangibles of $476.0M break down as $464.0M customer relationships
and $12.0M developed technology. Buying a business with customers produces a customer-relationships
intangible. Neither AI security deal produced one.

**What this supports, and where it stops.** These acquirers are buying technology and teams, and
the accounting says the revenue attached is small enough not to move the acquirer's statements.
"Not material" is a threshold relative to the buyer's own size, so it is an upper bound and not a
statement that the products earn nothing — Palo Alto runs roughly $9B of revenue, so its threshold
is high. The absent customer-relationships intangible means the acquirer assigned no separable
value to customer contracts, which is a valuation judgment rather than a headcount of customers.
Both points belong in any published read.

## Finding 3 — CyberArk is no longer an independent discloser

CyberArk filed Form 15-12G on 2026-02-23 (accession 0001104659-26-018360) deregistering its
securities, and its EDGAR record now carries no ticker. Its investor-relations and newsroom URLs
redirect to Palo Alto Networks' Idira identity platform page. Palo Alto's FY25 10-K confirms the
agreement was signed 2025-07-30 with an expected close in the second half of its fiscal 2026.

This touches live site data. The Identity domain card carries the watch item "Palo Alto vs CyberArk
mix," which can no longer resolve as written because the two are one filer. Its other watch item,
"First disclosed agentic-identity revenue," is now measurably harder: the four identity vendors
checked disclose nothing, and one of them has stopped filing.

## Not verified

**Cato Networks / Aim Security.** Cato is private, so no filing exists, and its newsroom sits
behind an Incapsula challenge that returned an empty JavaScript page on every attempt. The
challenge was not bypassed, per the standing rule on this project. Absence here says nothing about
whether the deal is real.

**CyberArk Q2/Q3 2025 earnings.** The lane could not locate standalone 6-K earnings filings for
that window and did not chase it, likely absorbed by the merger proxy process.

## Bounds on any read built from this

1. The null result covers written disclosure. It does not cover earnings-call transcripts, which
   is where the site's one existing buyer-side AI figure came from.
2. "Not material" is an upper bound relative to the acquirer's size, not zero.
3. The absent customer-relationships intangible is a valuation judgment, not a customer count.
4. Two named lists of acquisitions in this record are unconfirmed by the lead. Anything drawn from
   the second table needs re-extraction first.
