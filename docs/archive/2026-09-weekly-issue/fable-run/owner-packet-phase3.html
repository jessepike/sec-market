1. The design in one paragraph

The site already contains a good argument. It is in the wrong order. The signed judgment, what changed, what to do and avoid, what the site refuses to call, and the four conditions that would change the call all exist today, well written, between 4,700 and 7,000 pixels down a 15,000 pixel page, underneath twenty-three funding and filing cards. This design moves the argument to the top. That part is small, needs no new route and no new data, and is proposed as wave 1. The larger part (rows for cards, a page of its own for the thesis that grows one line per signed issue, a method page, one typeface, no dark panels) is proposed as wave 2, to start only after a second issue has been signed, because a thesis ledger and a redesign are worth little on a site that has published once. Section 11 gives the owner the choice between the full build, the two waves, and wave 1 alone.

2. Principles

  1. Argument before inventory. A security leader came for the read. The tracked items are the evidence for the read and sit below it.
  2. The thesis is a standing object, the issue is a weekly event. They look different and are never confused on screen.
  3. The thesis sentence never travels alone. Wherever it appears, for a person or a machine, it sits with its verdict, confidence, signer, date and what it does not claim. Intent requires claims to keep their uncertainty.
  4. Honest at small scale. One signed issue exists. Every history surface shows one row and says so.
  5. Rows, tables and plain labels. Linear's restraint, Zed's density. One accent. Light only.
  6. Accessibility is the thing to protect. The site scores 4 of 5 today with zero axe violations. Every stream re-runs the measurements in evidence/baseline/measure/ before merge.
  7. Keep the gates green. Section 9.

1. The position

10. The strongest case against monetizing at all

Every direct model earns about $11,000 on the central case, and the largest line is a day of the author's time, which he can sell in his actual job without building anything. Each model costs something that cannot be bought back. A licence gives an organization a relationship with the person who scores its segment, and whatever the policy says, he will know who pays when he writes the verdict. A supporter tier introduces a number that goes up and down, and such numbers change what people write, which intent forbids. A payment provider brings an owner gate, a tax question and a disclosure question at the employer, for four figures. A free, signed, unbought weekly judgment is rare because nobody is paying for it. On this view monetizing is value-destroying, because the publication's commercial function is to be free and every dollar taken directly reduces a much larger indirect return.

Where this plan lands. It accepts almost all of that: zero direct revenue in the 90-day path and zero in the cautious twelve-month case. It keeps the licence for one reason above the money. A sum this small cannot distort a judgment, and an organization willing to pay is the measurement this project has been unable to make. Two things end the commercial path outright: an answer to M-3 that makes outside payment awkward, or twelve signed issues with no unprompted organizational enquiry. In either case stop at stage 3 and keep the site as a free credibility asset.
