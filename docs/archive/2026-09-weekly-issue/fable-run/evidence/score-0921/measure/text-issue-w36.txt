What we now think

This week combined eight public-issuer earnings prints — CrowdStrike, Zscaler, SentinelOne, Okta, Rubrik, Palo Alto Networks, Broadcom and Nutanix all reported in-window — with thin early-stage funding and quiet, mostly undisclosed M&A. The pattern is consistent with the standing infrastructure-to-security lag call, not a change to it.

Thesis — holds: Every prints-lane figure this window is a strong beat with accelerating or steady growth (CrowdStrike net new ARR +51% YoY, Palo Alto Next-Gen Security ARR +63% YoY, Rubrik subscription ARR +33% YoY, NVIDIA Data Center revenue +117% YoY). That is evidence of demand strength at the infrastructure/platform layer, not a change to the standing infrastructure-to-security lag call: the call is about the gap between infrastructure spend and security-market translation, and this week’s prints are consistent with a call already built on strong infrastructure numbers. No M&A or funding item this window is large or structurally unusual enough to move it on its own.

CONFIDENCE · MEDIUM

CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks +2
Stable 0–8-quarter infrastructure-to-security lag: this window adds one mature infrastructure impulse and no comparable registered security response, so the lag stays a no-call rather than a measured number of quarters.
