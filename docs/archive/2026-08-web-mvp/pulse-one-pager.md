# Security Market Pulse

securitymarketpulse.com

## What it is

A standing read on where the cybersecurity market is going, built only from public information and
kept current.

It is there to help with timing. What is moving now, what is worth preparing for, and what looks
busy without being real. Every judgment on it is signed by a person and dated.

## What is on it

Four domain postures: Network and SASE, Data and AI security, Cloud security, and Identity. Each one
carries a plain read, the public numbers underneath it, a horizon, and a mark for how sure anyone is.

What moved, covering the prints, funding and deals that actually landed in the last few weeks. A
quiet week is reported as a quiet week.

Platform pressure, which sorts vendors into who is holding position, who is buying their way into a
category, and who is being outrun inside their own motion.

Tracked hypotheses, written down in advance and scored against evidence rather than opinion. Two of
the four currently read NO-CALL.

A scorecard of 42 vendor prints across eight domains. Private companies stay on the board as capital
raised or "no series" rather than being handed invented revenue.

An evidence library. Every figure links to the public document it came from.

## The rules it runs on

Public sources only. No client information and nothing confidential.

Every number traces to a document you can open and read yourself.

Facts and judgments are labeled separately. Tracked means a public print with no interpretation.
Judged means a person signed it and put a date on it.

When the evidence will not support a call, it says NO-CALL instead of making one up.

Not investment advice.

## What it says right now

Infrastructure is running well ahead. Security is splitting rather than booming. Appliance-led
network security is live today, selected data vendors are accelerating, and 2025 security budgets
did not follow the infrastructure build. Whether infrastructure spend reliably leads security spend
by a fixed number of quarters is still NO-CALL.

## How to use it

Five minutes on the front page tells you what changed and what it means. The rest is there when you
want to check the reasoning.

If a figure is wrong, or a read goes further than its evidence does, that is worth an email.
Corrections are more useful than agreement.
